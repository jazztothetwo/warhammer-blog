This is going to be a long one.

## Why Custodes and why now?

Custodes have always really interested me. An army of the Emperor's greatest, small model count, useable in both 40k and Heresy. The Custodes are the pinnacle of humanity. Standing above all, even the Astartes. A single Custodian is themself a near unstoppable army. They are only bested by the Emperor himself. What's not to love?

The models.

I didn't love the models.

The Custodes, as a playable army in both 40k and 30k, only came out in mid 2016. All things considered, they are a relatively recent faction and as such, one would expect their models to look fairly decent. And I'm sure at the time, pre Primaris, pre scale growth, they did.

I, however, started Warhammer during the Era Indomitus. My first introduction to Warhammer was with Primaris Space Marines. Once I learnt about Custodes, I still thought they were cool. But comparing them to Primaris? They looked, in my opinion, bad. They were squatty. They stood a head shorter than Primaris. There was a disconnect there. A disconnect between the Custodes of the lore and the models on the table. The models didn't make me excited. And as such, I continued on my Warhammer journey and left them behind.

To be clear, I still **loved** the Custodes whenever they would appear in art, stories, and animations. Episode 2 of The Tithes, about a Custodian and a Sister of Silence overseeing a Black Ship and collecting a tithe of human psykers, got me so incredibly hyped. But after watching that animation, I went to the Warhammer web store and... was immediately reminded why I wasn't already collecting Custodes.

Then, one fateful January morning in 2026, the New Year Preview dropped. And what did I see? But brand new Custodes of course. Suddenly the models matched what I'd always imagined Custodes should look like. But we weren't just greeted by new plastic Custodian Guard and Custodian Sentinels. There were brand new **plastic** Caladius Annihilator Grav-tanks. **Plastic** Contemptor-Achillus and Contemptor-Galatus. New and **plastic** EVERYTHING.

## My thoughts on the new models

[![The new Custodes](https://assets.warhammer-community.com/carousel-1a-rrmpdivx7w.png)](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/mdp0kzdk/the-new-year-preview-all-new-legio-custodes-revealed/)

*Image: Games Workshop / Warhammer Community. Click the image for the original reveal.*

What can I say? The new models are a drastic improvement.

The biggest improvement for me is their proportions. They're no longer squat. They stand tall, with poses that give them a proper sense of presence. Some are standing heroically, while others are taking a powerful forward step, or readying their weapon. They look imposing. They look like they could walk into a battlefield full of Space Marines and still be the most dangerous thing there.

That's what I felt was missing from the older models. The ornate armour and guardian spears were there, but the models underneath didn't quite sell the idea. These new sculpts do. There's finally a connection between the Custodes I see in art and animations and the ones I want to put on the table.

Their weapons have a more modern look, and the detailing is crisp. There's plenty to look at without losing that recognisable Custodes appearance. They're still covered in glorious, almost excessive ornamentation. Still carrying weapons that are as much a statement as they are a practical way of killing something. They're still Custodes. Just much closer to how I'd always pictured them.

And then there's the plastic.

It wasn't just new infantry that got announced, there was much more to get excited about. Seeing the dreadnought kits alongside them made this feel like it could be the start of an entire army project. Suddenly, I wasn't just looking at a model or unit I liked. I was thinking about infantry, Dreadnoughts, tanks, and how everything would look together. And all without a single resin model in sight.

Then, at the Big Summer Preview...

[![Telemon Heavy Dreadnought](https://assets.warhammer-community.com/articles/0-2026/june/wc-22-06/big-summer-preview/image1-wcqohqc2wu.jpg)](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/syfufbyf/the-big-summer-preview-new-plastic-custodians-incoming/)

*Image: Games Workshop / Warhammer Community. Click the image for the original reveal.*

Not in my wildest dreams did I think they would ever release a plastic Telemon. This was a model that I was debating buying in resin (weapons sold separately of course) just to have. And now it's in plastic, for a fraction of the cost, with all the weapon options, and perfectly detailed. It's fantastic.

However.

Like with anything, I did have a few criticisms of the new sculpts. And after putting together a box or two, I developed a few more.

The troops aren't as tall as I think they should be. While far better proportioned than their previous models, they aren't much taller. If you ignore the hair, they're barely taller than their predecessors. They’re substantially taller than Heresy Tactical Marines, even the new ones. But compared to a Primaris, they are **maybe** a few millimetres taller. And that’s with the helmets on. This is frustrating, but not a dealbreaker. They look fantastic in Heresy games, if a bit smaller than I envision in games of 40k.

The models also have a little too much detail. They look fantastic painted up by the Eavy Metal team on Warhammer Community. I worry that all that detail will look cluttered once I get my own paints on them.

When I got around to building my box of Custodian Guard and box of Custodian Sentinels I encountered another issue I had with the new models. The hair. Now, the instructions state that all six of the hair sculpts fit on any helmeted head, and the result will fit on any body. But that's just not accurate. The plumes of hair fit on every version of the helmet sure, but they absolutely do not fit on every body. I had issues with the hair clipping the cape, the shoulderpads, the weapon. It prevented the models from looking in any direction but dead forward. It sometimes caused the heads to tilt forward. In the end I added a small ball of two part putty to the necks of all my Custodians, just so the hair would behave slightly better.

The models are already extremely monopose, I don't understand why they went with the decision of letting the hair be customisable. So now instead of one head and hair piece fitting perfectly on each body, you have six heads and six hair pieces that don't fit well on *any* of the bodies. And it's a real misstep on GW's part.

But all that said, those aren't enough to dampen my excitement. For years, I loved the idea of collecting Custodes. Now I actually love the models I’m collecting.
