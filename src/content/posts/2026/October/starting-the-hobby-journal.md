Welcome to my Warhammer blog. I'm Jarrod, and this is where I'll keep a record of what I'm painting, collecting, playing, and working on.

The idea is simple: take a few photos, write down what happened, and give each project somewhere to live beyond the painting desk. Finished models will get their moment, but I want to make room for the steps along the way too. A colour scheme taking shape, a base coming together, or a model that still needs another evening of work are all worth an update.

## What you'll find here

This will be a hobby journal, with painting and collecting at the centre of it. I'll share work in progress, finished miniatures, and notes on the choices that went into them.

When I write about a paint scheme, I'll include enough detail to make it useful to come back to: which colours I used, how I applied them, and what I'd change next time. It's easy to look at a finished miniature and forget how it got there. Having those notes beside the photos should help when it's time to paint the next one.

There'll also be space for the less tidy parts of a project. If something needs repainting or an idea doesn't quite work, that belongs in the journal as well.

## Working through the pile

The pile of shame gets a mention on the homepage, so it deserves one here too.

I'd like this blog to be a reason to keep making progress. That might mean finishing a unit, sorting out a colour scheme, or getting a single model ready for paint. A smaller update is still something I can look back on and build from.

I'm leaving the schedule open for now. The aim is to keep a useful record as projects develop, with photos that show what changed between sessions.
![Part of the backlog of sons of horus.](https://images.jarrodscherer.au/2026/October/starting-the-hobby-journal/the-pile.webp)

## A starting point

This first post is an introduction. The posts that follow can get into the models themselves: what's on the desk, what I'm trying, and how it's coming along.

For now, welcome to the blog. Let's see how much of that pile makes it onto a painted base.
